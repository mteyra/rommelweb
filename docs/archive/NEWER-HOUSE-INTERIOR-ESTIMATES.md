# Newer houses: evidence and provisional interior estimates

Research reviewed **15–16 November 2026**. The resident has requested a logical first placement of single apartments and WG apartment blocks in **all six newer houses**, preserving their current outlines. These estimates are a separate step from the completed older-house layouts. Their purpose is to make a plausible arrangement reviewable on site; they do not establish surveyed rooms, current occupancy, or actual apartment numbers.

The newer houses are **49 Schwarz, 51a Blau, 53a Lila, 55a Grau, 57a Türkis and 59a Pink**, according to the [Rommelwood house register](https://rommelwood.de/wohnen/h%C3%A4user/). The retained exterior sections determine where geometry can fit. Unassigned space remains visible and gray. Room blocks may move or change after inspection, but the exterior must not be resized to make an assumed count fit.

The numbers have no explicit unit legend; centimetres are inferred from their coherent relationship to the annotated bed, desk, entrance and fittings. The **2.92 × 7.20 m rectangle is an apartment bounding interior envelope**. Its product is **21.024 m²**, including the area occupied by internal bathroom partitions and other features inside that envelope. It is not a measured usable-floor-area claim. The smaller **2.92 × 3.50 m = 10.22 m²** rectangle describes the living/sleeping zone, not a complete apartment.

The drawing includes the private bathroom, wardrobe/service passage and entry kitchenette. It supports a full single-apartment block much better than treating the living zone as the whole room. It does not give a current house/floor mapping, exterior wall construction, partition thickness or floor-to-floor height. The accompanying page explicitly allows mirrored rooms and independently moved windows.

**Working single dimensions:** use approximately **2.92 × 7.20 m** for a typical bounding interior block wherever the preserved shell admits it. The selected **3.12 m repeating pitch = 2.92 + 0.10 + 0.10 m** reserves an estimated 0.10 m on either side of each block, producing 0.20 m between adjacent interiors. That allowance is a provisional design choice, not a measured newer-house partition. Do not carry over the older houses' resident-selected 3.10 m bay automatically or add another full wall thickness on top of this gap.

## What can be inferred about WG sizes

If ten groups contain only three or four residents and together accommodate 38, the arithmetic is:

```text
x + y = 10
3x + 4y = 38
x = 2 three-person groups
y = 8 four-person groups
```

This establishes a **complex-wide group-size mix under the stated three/four-person premise**. It does not identify a building, floor or doorway. The G1/G2 category counts do not independently assign a category to each group.

Multiplying the per-place category averages gives useful area budgets:

| Group size | Category-area budget | Candidate whole-apartment block |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Three residents | 3 × 23.7–25.6 = **71.1–76.8 m²** | Approximately **70 m²** |
| Four residents | 4 × 23.7–25.6 = **94.8–102.4 m²** | Approximately **93 m²** |

The candidate blocks are deliberately below those attributed category totals, leaving some allowance for shared building facilities that are outside the apartment. The deduction is a **modeling assumption**, because the operator does not publish the actual communal-area allocation. It is not a formula for converting rental category areas into surveyed net areas.

One consistent starting geometry is:

| Candidate | Width × depth | Block area | Relationship to the room sketch |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Three-person WG | **8.76 × 8.00 m** | **70.08 m²** | Three 2.92 m width modules; 8.00 m depth is estimated |
| Four-person WG | **11.68 × 8.00 m** | **93.44 m²** | Four 2.92 m width modules; 8.00 m depth is estimated |

These are **candidate fitting dimensions**, not mandatory rectangles or measured WG plans. Rotation or a different aspect ratio at comparable area may fit the existing shell and circulation better. Keep a WG as one apartment block with one shared entrance during this pass. There is no verified bedroom dimension, bathroom subdivision, kitchen position or internal door schedule for the actual newer WGs.

For a reasonableness check only, a hypothetical 2.92 × 4.60 m bedroom would occupy about 13.43 m². Three such bedrooms leave about 29.78 m² within the 70.08 m² block for shared kitchen/living space, bathrooms and internal circulation; four leave about 39.71 m² within the 93.44 m² block. This illustrates that the blocks can contain a plausible shared program. **The 4.60 m bedroom depth and those internal subdivisions are not part of the public-source evidence and should not be presented as confirmed rooms.**

The two accessible singles have only the operator's 30 m² category reference. Their exact shape and location remain unverified. The selected **5.00 × 6.00 m = 30.00 m² bounding block** is a design allowance, not a measured accessible apartment or an exact conversion of the category figure. It does not imply that an accessible route, door clearance, bathroom arrangement or turning space has been checked.

## What the buildings and marker counts support

The [architect's completed-project description](https://www.karlundp.de/projekt/wohnanlage-fuer-studierende-erlangen/) gives six roughly **23 × 23 m**, **three- to eight-storey** buildings. It records 372 singles, including two accessible units, and 38 places in ten WGs. Residential upper-floor plans are nearly identical, with varying loggias and exterior safety stairs. Ground floors contain a mixture of bicycle, technical, storage, laundry and communal functions, plus two caretaker apartments. Concrete structure, lightweight internal partitions, prefabricated wet rooms and no basement are described.

That supports repeating a sensible residential pattern on comparable upper sections, while treating ground floors and the communal building separately. It does not establish the location of every WG, and “nearly identical” does not mean every floor has an identical usable outline or loggia.

The retained public marker dataset supplies this aggregate coverage:

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |

| 53a | 4 | 14 | 56 |
| 55a | 5 | 14 | 70 |

There is no corresponding marker coverage for the newer ground floors or 51a. The dataset's own coverage qualification says modern group-living rooms may be absent. Consequently:

- Fourteen is a useful **covered-room-count guide** for ordinary tower upper floors.
- Fourteen markers do **not** establish that every such floor contains only singles, or that no WG exists upstairs.
- The subtraction **372 − 364 = 8** identifies eight single places not accounted for by that interpretation of marker coverage. It does not locate them on ground floors or in 51a.
- Marker coordinates do not supply partition lines or door positions; some retained points fall outside the exterior. This estimate uses aggregate counts, not individual marker identities.

For scale, fourteen 2.92 × 7.20 m single envelopes occupy **294.34 m²**. An idealized 23 × 23 m footprint is 529 m², leaving approximately 234.66 m² before accounting for the actual footprint, walls, loggias, stairs and circulation. This is a plausibility check, not a space entitlement. The real retained floor section, including cutouts, governs every placement.

Its [small site-plan floor diagrams](https://www.karlundp.de/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/Erlangen500-Lageplan.jpg) therefore cannot be registered directly as current floors.

A “five groups on each Blue upper floor” arrangement happens to reproduce the 38-place arithmetic if each floor has four four-person groups and one three-person group. That is only a scenario. The current footprint and circulation may not admit it, and the arithmetic supplies no evidence that the real rooms are there. Do not force either this distribution or the complex-wide published total into the preserved outlines.

### Fitting method and independent checks

The [generator](../../scripts/new-house-module-layout.mjs) builds a rigid orthonormal frame for each house from its retained source facade. The internal **22.30 m square is a conservative fitting frame**, centered inside the source footprint; it does not become a replacement floor outline. The complete source floor section, including its actual boundary, remains the display floor.

Ordinary floors try perimeter-bank patterns such as **7 + 4 + 3**, **7 + 3 + 4**, and **4 + 4 + 4 + 2**, with mirrored arrangements and limited shifts **along** facades. Full apartment modules are either retained or omitted; they are not clipped, stretched, shortened or moved into the middle of the floor to recover a count. Horizontal recess slabs from the existing exterior detail model provide the per-floor protected loggia, stair and ground-frontage polygons.

Each house keeps the same estimated **3.00 × 5.40 m stair allowance** and **2.20 × 2.20 m lift/risers allowance** at identical coordinates on all its floors, behind the selected stair frontage. These are planning reservations, not surveyed stair or lift dimensions. The hallway uses connected 1.40 m strips. Source windows, exterior details, floor elevations and older-house studies remain separate; this fitting pass changes none of them.

Independent review of all 34 generated floors checked:

- Every standard single remains a full **2.92 × 7.20 m** rectangle. Whole WG and accessible-type estimates retain their stated dimensions.
- Apartment modules, core reservations and hall polygons do not overlap one another or the retained protected recess polygons.
- Core polygons remain identical between levels of each house, and sampled points across every entrance segment lie on both the apartment/core edge and hallway edge.
- A separate hallway check sampled possible passage centers at 0.10 m spacing, keeping at least 0.70 m from every corridor boundary. Its connected 1.40 m clearance network reaches all apartment and core approaches on all 34 floors. This verifies the schematic circulation geometry at that sampling resolution, not an accessible route or an as-built evacuation plan.
- Every schematic facade-facing window segment ends within **0.29 m of the retained source-floor exterior**, consistent with the fitting frame's perimeter allowance. This establishes facade proximity, not correspondence to a particular photographed window.

The last distinction matters. For example, Lila's fitted 4. OG banks place seven candidates along the NNE side and three along the SSW side, whereas the exterior photo row targets contain five NNE and six SSW openings. The photographs do not establish a one-window-per-apartment mapping, but this difference shows why the provisional room-bank rhythm must not be described as verified window alignment. Actual window-to-apartment relationships remain an explicit field check; the folded facade keeps its independent appearance model.

Unassigned source-floor area stays gray. It includes protected exterior recesses and other unallocated areas; gray does not mean a confirmed vacant room, a complete nonresidential program or an absent floor slab. Separate narrow wall bands show the estimated side allowances. The published total is a comparison target, never a reason to shrink that gray area or modify the retained outline.

## Implementation and review boundary

The following facts and assumptions need to remain distinct in the generated records and the website:

| Item | Status |

The model should expose the proposed room/apartment blocks without adopting real apartment numbers. A shared apartment counts as **one selectable apartment block** and separately records its estimated three/four-person capacity; it must not be mistaken for one single bedroom or inflated into four independent front doors. The source drawing is linked as evidence; third-party artwork is not bundled or traced into the website.

### Field checks that will most improve the estimate

1. Identify every WG by **house and floor only**, and distinguish three from four bedrooms. Check Blue 51a's proposed three groups per upper floor and the four selected tower EGs first; these are the largest distribution uncertainties.
2. Check **Grau 55a / 2. OG and 5. OG** around the southwest loggia. Establish how the real apartments fit there before restoring either omitted candidate or changing dimensions.
3. Confirm the actual entrance facade, lobby-to-core connection and corridor direction for each house. Measure a representative hallway against the tentative 1.40 m assumption.
4. For one ordinary tower upper floor, confirm the number of single-apartment entrances and whether a WG shares that floor. Measure one full entry-to-window depth against the 7.20 m sketch and its interior width against 2.92 m. Match facade windows to apartments, especially Lila's differing fitted and photographed row rhythms.
5. On each ground floor, distinguish residential space, caretaker accommodation, bicycle/service space and common rooms, especially the unassigned residential program at 59a and 51a. Locate the two accessible singles if observable without occupant information.
6. Measure a representative partition and stair/lift core, then a whole WG perimeter before subdividing it. Mark loggia cutouts and actual exterior-window alignments per floor.
7. Check whether the operator's additional M1 category corresponds to an existing two-person apartment, and which house/floor contains it. This resolves the 410/412 capacity discrepancy without inventing geometry.

Resident names, occupancy, personal contacts and access information are unnecessary for these physical-layout checks.
