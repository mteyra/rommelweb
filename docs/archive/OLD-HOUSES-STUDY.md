The variable room dimensions and category-average fitting below describe the earlier model; they do not define the current layouts. House 55 has since received its own three resident-approved module floors.

15 November 2026. The live map contains the official exterior models of all eleven houses. This revision adds provisional interiors to all three floors of the five older houses. These are **partial studies**, not measured floor plans or a claim that the complete dormitory has been reconstructed.

The distinction matters: calculation can disprove a candidate, or show that it fits its assumptions. It cannot establish that unobserved walls exist. No statistical model or survey supports “95% certain” for these interiors.

## Fitting the other houses

The generator retains each house's own official shell and all three independent source sections. A reviewed boundary correspondence locates the comparable room banks and circulation core. Rooms are then rebuilt with **straight dividers and fronts**, facing their own source façade; the warped room polygons from a direct transfer are not used.

The straight-bank calculation uses an explicit **0.20 m exterior allowance** and **0.10 m allocation on each side of a shared boundary**. Endpoint allowances distinguish an exterior end from a hall/stair allocation boundary. The available frontage determines the equal conditional clear widths. Depth follows from the benchmark:

```text
conditional clear depth = 16.4 m² / conditional clear width
```

This does not assume the earlier common width or depth is correct. It does still assume a particular wall allocation and room count. Counts in the outer banks are selected from frontage plausibility and compared with the public index; central three-room banks are explicitly borrowed from House 55 as hypotheses.

An accepted bank must remain within its source region, avoid other assigned spaces and leave the declared approach behind its room doors. The generator's **1.40 m gross rectangular approach**, approximately **1.20 m after two 0.10 m allocations**, is a study guard, not a building-code assessment. It may retreat a bank's depth by at most **0.20 m**, recording the resulting area shortfall. It does not change the official exterior to manufacture a fit.

“On each floor” describes the repeated study core. **Additional lower-floor wings remain unassigned**; the table does not assert that the ground and first floors contain only those rooms. Upper-floor room/window observations from House 55 are not copied into the other houses or lower floors. Their study windows, possible-wall bands and dimension guides are not invented.

In Houses 51 and 53, three central rooms per bank require roughly **6.8–7.0 m gross depth** under this particular area calculation. Even after the full 0.20 m retreat, the minimum source-contained approach is only **0.651 / 0.815 m for House 51 L/R** and **0.752 / 0.899 m for House 53 L/R**. All four would also fail a relaxed **1.20 m gross** comparison. Those banks receive no invented individual partitions or doors. This rejects the borrowed candidate under its assumptions; a different count, apartment type, wall allowance or measured circulation arrangement could change the result.

House 59's east frontage supports competing width/count interpretations. Choosing four typical-width bays or three wider bays simply to match a total would conceal that ambiguity, so the bank remains undivided.

The accepted target-house envelopes are mostly **16.400 m²** under their declared allowances. Bounded depth retreats leave **House 57 N at about 16.029 m²**, **57 L at 16.317 m²**, and **59 L at 15.987 m²**. They remain visible as conditional candidates with their shortfalls recorded.

Area compatibility does not validate the published room sketch. In particular, House 57's N bank has an adopted conditional clear width of about **2.473 m**. N2's gross enclosing strip is only about **2.673 m**, already narrower than the sketch's approximately **2.70 m** living-zone short side. That exact sketch cannot simply be inserted there. A narrower real apartment, a different bank count or different wall assumptions remain possible; none is proved by the current drawing. The preliminary width range used to compare counts is not a guarantee that the final, explicitly allocated width matches that sketch.

## Rejected candidate and independent checks

The first transfer used a continuous piecewise affine mapping throughout House 55's rooms. It could preserve enclosure and non-overlap while bending a rectangular apartment into an implausible shape. For example, the earlier mapped L1 gross areas in Houses 51 and 53 were about **12.29 and 11.51 m²**. That version was rejected.

Its [input](../evidence/rejected-old-house-pwa-input.json), [metadata](../evidence/rejected-old-house-pwa-metadata.json), [independent audit](../evidence/rejected-old-house-pwa-audit.json) and [comparison plot](../screenshots/older-houses/rejected-old-house-pwa-overview.png) are retained as evidence. They are not live floor inputs.

The final [independent audit](../evidence/old-house-studies-audit.json) reconstructs source-plane sections separately from the JavaScript generator, then checks containment, intersections, area accounting, room shapes, saved-door adjacency, circulation components and inventory gaps across all fifteen floors. The [overview plot](../screenshots/older-houses/old-house-studies-overview.png) makes the unassigned regions visible.

All fifteen floors pass those numerical checks. They contain **258 provisional room references**, compared with **339 public index entries**. **147 room references have no represented connection to a stairwell** in the saved door graph. That is missing model evidence, not a finding that real residents lack access. All displayed doorways pass the separate local 1.20 m gross approach-circle check; no bent-room flags remain.

Across the fifteen provisional floor planes, **3376.704 m²** lies outside all study spaces and **808.618 m²** is explicitly unassigned, for a combined **4185.322 m²**. These source-section areas include roof margins and unexplored wings; they are **not habitable floor area**. Their size makes it especially inappropriate to describe the repeated cores as complete floor plans.

Containment within each saved floor outline uses the existing **0.20 mm computational bound**. The separate source-plane reconstruction compares area with a perimeter-scaled 0.20 mm bound and also requires boundary offsets below **1.0 mm**, accounting for source-face quantization and a different triangulation. These are distinct checks. Derived coordinates are serialized at 10⁻⁹ m and shared interior boundaries are noded at 10⁻⁷ m with exact polygon-clipping predicates. Tiny inferred hall appendages with a mouth no wider than 0.002 m and an individual area no greater than 0.01 m² are left unassigned. The source sections are unchanged. None of these bounds describes survey accuracy.

The west doorway is observed only in House 55's upper study. Its transfer elsewhere is an estimate. The saved door graph does **not** establish an eastern connection to the stairwell in any of these candidates. A connected polygon or an available route probe must not be mistaken for a verified entrance or evacuation route.

Door adjacency and usable local approach are checked separately. House 57's copied stair opening failed the finite-width approach check and is omitted; its actual stair access remains unknown. “West hall” and “East hall” in the transferred studies are labels inherited from the House 55 drawing, not geographic compass directions after rotation.

## Optional apartment arrangements

Selecting a study room offers **Explore a layout concept**. It isolates the apartment, moves the camera closer and shows a possible arrangement at metre scale. It does not enlarge furniture or change the room to make a layout fit.

The bed **0.97 × 2.17 m**, desk **0.75 × 1.55 m** and wardrobe **1.00 × 0.60 m** are approximate footprints read from the published sketch. The kitchenette **0.60 × 1.20 m** and bath **1.30 × 1.50 m** are chosen examples. Their placement, mirror, partitions, heights and plumbing remain unverified.

A conservative inscribed rectangle, boundary/obstacle checks, a **0.75 m swept walking path** and a **0.90 m entry landing** decide whether the illustrative arrangement fits. These are display-study guards, not accessibility checks. A checked dogleg arrangement can fit all five items in the House 55 candidates, but its bath encroaches on the separate 3.30 m living-depth reference; that compromise is disclosed in Layout notes. The module never claims to have reproduced the complete published room sketch.

An unavailable or partial fit is reported rather than shrinking the furniture. The original study and its doors remain unchanged. No resident identity, occupancy, private invitation or messaging data is introduced.

The release checks cover all **258** provisional room envelopes: **192 full examples, 42 partial examples and 24 unavailable layouts**. The sixty repeated House 55 room references all fit the five-item example under these guards. This tally measures the algorithm's current arrangement options; a failed example does not prove that the real apartment cannot be furnished.
