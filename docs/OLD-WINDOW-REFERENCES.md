## Typical window dimensions

The older single-room sketch says the window extends from approximately “95” to the ceiling, horizontally from “60 cm” to “165” from the left. It also gives a continuous ceiling height of “250”.

These annotations support the following **approximate typology**, with centimetres read consistently across the sketch:

| Quantity | Approximate value | How it is obtained |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Sill above room floor | 0.95 m | The sketch's window-height note |
| Window horizontal extent | 1.05 m | 1.65 m minus 0.60 m |
| Window vertical extent | 1.55 m | 2.50 m clear ceiling minus 0.95 m sill |
| Room clear ceiling | 2.50 m | Explicit ceiling note |

These describe the approximate extent indicated by a resident drawing. They do not distinguish glazing size, frame outside dimensions and structural rough opening with construction accuracy. The 2.50 m ceiling dimension is **not** floor-to-floor height. Neither that value nor the 2.70 m living-zone width establishes façade bay pitch.

## Character to reproduce

The regular older windows are upright rectangles, with a less slender proportion than the newer floor-to-ceiling windows. The clear operator detail shows two vertically stacked panes separated by **one horizontal crossbar around mid-height**. Its exact height and frame widths are unmeasured. A universal vertical center mullion or the newer buildings' deep dark head-strip treatment is not established for this regular type.

The photographed room-window stacks generally align vertically. Use an aligned illustrative rhythm as a starting point, interrupted by actual wall edges, recesses and roof cutoffs. Small end walls may have one column while larger wings have several. This is not evidence for a fixed count or one common pitch on all five houses.

Ground-level regular windows are visible, so excluding every older-house ground row would omit part of the photographed character. Ground floors also have broad multi-pane entrance/shared glazing and other uses. A generic room-window profile cannot accurately reconstruct those larger openings or identify the room behind a candidate position.

The operator images visibly contain yellow, orange-toned and red frames against brown plate cladding. The resident map identifies the houses as Yellow 51, Orange 53, Red 55, White 57 and Green 59, but a nickname is not by itself a paint specification. This research has not established a current, address-registered color inventory for every window on all five houses. A muted observed frame cue or a clearly illustrative tint based on the house identity is acceptable; do not claim exact paint colors or universal consistency.

## Roof and floor safeguards

1. Keep the official LoD2 coordinates, scale, source wall polygons, holes and roof silhouette unchanged. Generate appearance graphics separately.
2. Require the **entire** candidate opening, including frame/sill extent, to fit the available wall region. Reject it if clipping would produce a triangular, severed or shortened window at a sloping roof or stepped wall edge.
3. A complete rectangle fitting an exterior wall is necessary for plausible appearance, but **does not prove occupied space behind it**. Keep the resident's unoccupied roof-triangle observation explicit. Do not describe generated upper windows as evidence for an extra room or storey.
4. Keep window-row elevations provisional until a measured floor section is available. Do not derive actual floors from the roof peak or substitute the typical clear ceiling height for storey height.
5. Do not infer dormers, skylights, doors or large shared-room glazing merely to fill a blank surface. The source envelope and a reviewed face reference must guide those features.
6. Keep all procedural opening counts and candidate positions separate from the approved room index and private community data.

The next useful evidence is one address-and-direction photo set for an older house, paired with its floor elevations or a dimensioned section showing the roof void. That would allow the current typology cues to become an actual façade inventory without changing the underlying source geometry.

## Current illustrative settings

The appearance study uses the approximately 1.05 × 1.55 m typology, 0.95 m sill, aligned columns and one middle transom. Its 3.25 m bay pitch and 0.08 m containment clearance are **rendering settings**, not surveyed dimensions. It does not add the newer buildings' dark head strip.

For this appearance layer, three provisional rows are distributed using a dominant broad horizontal source roof as a guide, rather than extending rows toward the highest roof peak. Geometry heuristics suppress low wings and candidate regions treated as roof-only; those heuristics do not classify actual use. This guide does not establish actual floor levels, headroom or occupancy, and does not change the separate floor-section data or official exterior.
